CELL : 
Cellularity of the marrow clot section refers to the proportion of hematopoietic (blood-forming) cells versus fat in a bone marrow sample. It helps assess marrow activity and disease status. In leukemia, cellularity changes can indicate remission or relapse.

It reflects how active the marrow is in producing blood cells.
- Normal Range: Varies with age—young individuals typically have higher cellularity (~80%), while older adults may have lower (~30%).

SMEAR:
Smear Differential Percentage of Blasts” Mean

proportion of immature white blood cells (blasts) seen in a peripheral blood smear
A higher percentage of blasts generally indicates more aggressive disease and a lower chance of remission, while a lower percentage is associated with better treatment response and higher likelihood of remission

- High blast percentage → indicates uncontrolled proliferation of immature cells.
- Low blast percentage → suggests better marrow recovery and treatment response.

INFIL:

Absolute Marrow Leukemia Cell Infiltrate

The absolute marrow leukemia cell infiltrate percentage (INFIL) measures how much of the bone marrow is occupied by leukemic cells. 
Higher infiltrate percentages generally reduce the likelihood of remission, 
while lower percentages are associated with better treatment response and higher remission probability

Example: If 70% of marrow cells are leukemic blasts, INFIL = 70%.

LI:
what is percentage labeling index of the bone marrow leukemia cells & how it will affect leukemia remission occurrenc

The percentage labeling index (LI) of bone marrow leukemia cells measures the fraction of leukemic blasts actively synthesizing DNA (i.e., proliferating). 
A higher LI indicates more aggressive disease and lowers the probability of remission, 
while a lower LI suggests slower cell turnover and increases the chance of remission.

BLAST:

The absolute number of blasts in the peripheral blood is the 
count of immature white blood cells (blasts) circulating outside the bone marrow. 

Higher blast counts indicate more aggressive leukemia and are associated with a lower probability of remission, 
while lower blast counts improve the likelihood of remission.

TEMP:

Quick answer: The highest temperature prior to the start of treatment is a clinical variable recorded in leukemia patients before therapy begins. 
Higher pre-treatment fevers usually indicate infection or systemic disease activity and are associated with a lower probability of remission, 
while lower or normal temperatures are linked to better remission outcomes
